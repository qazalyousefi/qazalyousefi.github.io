How I work

Same four steps for a small checkout change or a whole new screen size. What changes is how much evidence I need before committing.

1

Listen

Interviews, support tickets and analytics, weighed against business goals. I look for where what people say and what they do don't match.

Torob50 interviews and 12 months of behavioural data, plus 57K search queries: 37% described a need, not a product, so search alone wasn’t enough.

2

Frame

Turn it into one problem, one success metric and a clear hypothesis the team signs up to, plus what we're deliberately not solving.

BasalamOne outcome, PDP conversion, mapped in an opportunity solution tree. Seller-side causes went to the vendor team; we kept the buyer experience.

3

Shape

Explore wide, then cut hard. Prototype the riskiest assumption first and test with real users on the real device: phone, laptop or TV.

TorobCode-based prototypes of the map and store cards, tested on real phones. 6 out of 6 participants backed a denser card with a faster path to contact.

4

Ship

Scope the smallest release that proves it, write specs with engineers rather than for them, and measure against the metric from step 2.

Myket TVStaged rollout from 5% to 50% to 100% of users, with every step measured in Firebase against the event taxonomy we wrote with engineering.

What I believe

Good design is a decision, not decoration. It balances what people need, what the business needs and what we can actually build, and makes the hardest trade-off feel like the obvious one.

In-Person Shopping Discovery Journey

User Interviews – February 2025

1. Problem Statement

The purpose of this research is to uncover usability issues, user pain points, unmet needs, and the underlying reasons behind user churn in offline journey through qualitative interviews.

2. Research Goals

A. Validate Quantitative Findings Through Qualitative Research

This research aims to explain the patterns discovered in product analytics by combining quantitative data with qualitative user interviews.

Research Questions
  • At which stages of the journey do users churn the most, and why?
  • Do users need more product information, such as descriptions or specifications?
  • Are users concerned about inventory availability or pricing?
  • Do users lack trust in sellers?
  • Are users looking for seller information that they cannot find?
  • What information do users expect on the Product Detail Page (PDP)?
  • What are users looking for on the Vendor Listing Page (VLP)?
  • What is the conversion rate from entering the location flow to reaching the In-Person Shopping Home page?
  • Where does the highest drop-off occur throughout the journey?
  • Do users experience interaction problems with the map?
  • What information or functionality do users expect during location discovery?
  • Do users struggle with product variety?
  • Where are the highest numbers of dead clicks occurring, and what were users expecting to happen?
  • Are there any usability or performance issues?
  • What insights can be extracted from user complaints?
  • Where do technical bugs most frequently affect the user experience?

B. Discover User Needs (Qualitative Research)

  • Identify users' needs, pain points, motivations, and desires throughout the product discovery journey.
  • Understand how users discover physical stores.
  • Improve overall user satisfaction.
  • Increase returning users.
  • Increase loyal users.

C. Business Goals

Business Objectives
  • Acquire and retain users by providing an intuitive shopping experience.
  • Increase View-to-Buyer conversion rate.
  • Differentiate Torob from competitors through superior comparison experiences.
  • Reduce purchase friction and increase contact conversions.
  • Convert new users into buyers.

D. Feature Optimization

Design and prioritize new product capabilities based on:

  • Qualitative interview findings
  • Quantitative behavioral analytics

The ultimate goal is to create more value throughout the In-Person Shopping Journey while strengthening Torob's differentiation within the shopping market.

3. Research Conditions

Participants

Approximately 8 participants per user segment.

This sample size is expected to reveal recurring behavioral patterns and major usability issues.

Interview Duration

10–20 minutes

Interview Method

  • Remote interviews
  • Phone interviews

Required Tools

  • Voice recorder
  • Note-taking template
  • Interview guide

User Segmentation

Participants will be recruited from the following groups:

  • Users who viewed the journey but performed no meaningful action.
  • Users who churned after the Location step before reaching the Home page.
  • Users who dropped off on the Vendor Listing Page (VLP).
  • Users who never converted from VLP to Store Profile or PDP.
  • Users who viewed PDP but never interacted with shopping CTAs.
  • Users who viewed Store Profile but never interacted.
  • Users who submitted questions through the Q&A feature.
  • Users from different shopping categories.
  • Users who never returned within 13 days after viewing the In-Person Shopping Journey.

Data Source

Internal product database

Recruitment Method

  • Random phone calls
  • Scheduled interviews with loyal users

Recruitment Criteria

The goal is behavioral diversity, not demographic diversity.

Therefore, participant selection is not restricted by:

  • Age
  • Gender
  • Occupation
  • Device type

Target Users

People who prefer reliable in-person shopping experiences because they value:

  • Physical inspection before purchase
  • Expert consultation
  • High-quality products

Primary purchase motivations include:

  • Finding the right store
  • Product variety
  • Receiving consultation
  • Asking questions before buying
  • Store proximity
  • Immediate availability
  • Shopping within a specific area
  • Competitive pricing

Post-Interview Review

Immediately after each interview:

  • Verify the recording.
  • Evaluate interview quality.
  • Review participant responses.
  • Identify questions that need improvement.
  • Determine whether the interview answered the research objectives.

Additional Sources of Qualitative Feedback

Beyond interviews, qualitative insights can also be collected through:

  • The in-app Q&A feature
  • User feedback on social media
  • Surveys to validate interview findings at scale
  • Co-creation workshops with active users and internal team members

7. Data Analysis & Insight Generation

Step 1 — Organize Research Data

Document:

  • User needs
  • Pain points
  • Behaviors
  • Quotes
  • Assumptions

Step 2 — Identify Opportunities

Highlight recurring:

  • Needs
  • Pain points
  • Frustrations
  • Desired outcomes

Step 3 — Coding

Group similar interview observations into thematic codes.

Step 4 — Affinity Mapping

Cluster related codes into broader themes to reveal behavioral patterns.

Step 5 — Ideation & Decision Making

Generate solution ideas based on identified themes and prioritize future product improvements.

Opportunity Prioritization Checklist

Before prioritizing any opportunity, ask:

  • Is this a real user problem?
  • Is it feasible given our resources?
  • Will solving it create measurable value?
  • Can success be measured?
  • Does the team agree on its importance?

POV Madlib

After identifying key themes, use the POV Madlib framework to redefine problems from the user's perspective and generate human-centered design opportunities.

Mixed-Method Questionnaire

Following qualitative interviews, hypotheses should be validated with a larger sample using a mixed-method questionnaire.

Objectives

  • Validate interview findings.
  • Estimate the prevalence of discovered user needs and pain points.
  • Prioritize hypotheses before implementation.

Design Success Metrics

How we know the redesign works. Each metric has a clear formula and data source, mostly events from the tracking plan above, so it can be measured from day one.

North Star

Weekly plays per active viewer

One number that reflects both easy discovery and a reason to come back — the two goals of Myket TV.

Formulaplayer_viewed ÷ weekly active viewers
Business linkSubscriber base & retention

Usability

Can users find and play content with a D-pad, without friction?

    Share of Detail-page visits that turn into playback.

    Plays ÷ Detail views
    content_detail_viewed → player_viewed
  • Search success↑

    Searches that end on a result the user opens.

    Result taps ÷ Search sessions
    search_viewed → search_result_tapped
  • Bounce rate↓

    Sessions that leave Home without opening any title.

Outcome & Learnings

Impact

  • Increased subscription purchases on Myket’s VOD service
  • Gave Myket a TV app on par with Filimo, Namava and Filmnet

What I delivered

  • End-to-end UX & UI for the Android TV app: Home, Search, Detail, Player, Login and Subscription
  • A TV design system with colors, typography, icons, illustrations, components and D-pad states
  • A phone hand-off flow for login and payment, via QR code or short link
  • A tracking plan of 67 events, each tied to a business question, KPI and priority
  • An interactive prototype and a usability test plan

What I learned

  1. On TV, input is the bottleneck. The best fix for typing with a remote is to not type on the TV at all.
  2. Design the focus state first. With a D-pad, “where am I?” matters more than any visual detail.
  3. Define events with the design, not after. Writing the tracking plan alongside the UI made every decision measurable from day one.
